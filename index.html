Tender Readiness Checklist for Australian Microbusinesses: 17 Things to Fix Before You Chase a Grant or Tender

Before you spend a weekend writing a grant application or tender response, check the specific requirements first — then make sure your basic admin material is organised.

A lot of small operators do not lose time because they are bad at their work. They lose time because the boring proof is scattered everywhere: insurance certificates, project examples, capability statements, licences, policies, timelines, references, and the awkward little documents buyers suddenly expect when money is on the table.

This checklist is a quick self-check for Australian microbusinesses and sole traders. It will not tell you whether you will win anything. It will help you spot gaps before a deadline turns into a paperwork goblin with a knife.

Quick disclaimer: This is general educational/admin information only. It is not legal, financial, procurement, grant, tax, safety, workplace, insurance, accounting, or compliance advice. Always check the exact requirements of the opportunity and get qualified advice where needed.

The 17-point readiness checklist

  1. Your business details are current. Check your ABN/business name, public contact details, service area, and basic online presence. If a buyer/reviewer looks you up, the basics should not look abandoned.
  2. You can explain what you do in one sentence. If your description needs five paragraphs and a whiteboard, tighten it. A reviewer should quickly understand who you help and what you provide.
  3. Your services/products are clearly listed. Vague businesses are hard to buy from. List the specific services/products you actually want to sell.
  4. You have a draft capability statement. It does not need to be perfect, but you should have a short document covering what you do, who you help, relevant experience, strengths, contact details, and key credentials.
  5. You know which opportunities are a fit. Not every grant/tender is worth chasing. Define what is in-scope for your business before opportunity FOMO eats your week.
  6. You can provide insurance certificates where relevant. Know what insurance you hold, where the certificate is saved, and when it expires.
  7. You know your required licences/registrations. If your industry requires licences, registrations, permits, or qualifications, have proof ready and current.
  8. You have examples of past work. Prepare short project examples: what the customer needed, what you delivered, and what changed/resulted.
  9. You have references/testimonials where appropriate. Do not scramble for proof at the last minute. Track who can provide a reference or what testimonial can be used.
  10. Your basic policy documents are not a mystery. Depending on the opportunity, buyers may ask about safety, quality, privacy, environment, or other process documents. Know what you have and what is missing.
  11. You know your delivery capacity. Can you actually deliver if successful? Check staffing, time, equipment, location, subcontractors, and cash flow.
  12. You understand the deadline. A deadline is not just the final day. Work backwards: questions, document gathering, drafting, review, pricing, upload/submission.
  13. You understand mandatory requirements. Mandatory means mandatory. If you cannot meet a required condition, stop and get advice before wasting time.
  14. You have checked how you would price the work, including time, costs, risks, and assumptions. Do not win work that loses money. Check labour, materials, admin, travel, compliance, payment terms, and delivery risk.
  15. You know where your evidence is stored. Create one evidence folder or index. If every application starts with a search through Downloads, the machine is already eating you.
  16. You have a bid/no-bid process. Before starting, decide: proceed, fix gaps first, skip, or get advice. This prevents panic-bidding on bad-fit opportunities.
  17. You have enough time to submit properly. If the deadline is too close and the documents are not ready, skipping may be smarter than a rushed submission.

Simple readiness score

  • 0–5: fix basics before chasing opportunities seriously
  • 6–10: some foundation exists, but gaps may slow you down
  • 11–14: close to ready; focus on missing evidence and fit
  • 15–17: stronger starting position, but still check each opportunity carefully

This is a rough self-check only, not advice or a guarantee.
